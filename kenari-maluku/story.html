Our Story

Born in the
Spice Islands.

The Kenari nut didn't begin as a product. It began as a lifeline — the "tree of life" for Maluku families for centuries.

OUR PURPOSE

To Show the World
What Maluku Has
Always Known.

NATSEPA was born from a simple conviction: the Kenari nut — Canarium indicum — is one of the world's most extraordinary foods, yet almost no one outside Maluku knows it exists.

While walnut and almond dominate global shelves, Maluku's ancient forest quietly produces something rarer, richer, and more soulful. NATSEPA exists to change that — by honoring traditional practices, empowering local communities, and crafting premium products that let the Kenari speak for itself.

"The Kenari doesn't need us. We need it — to remember where real food comes from."

— NATSEPA, Ambon Maluku
Wild · Never Planted
HISTORY

A Tree That Outlived
Empires.

The Kenari tree has witnessed the full arc of Maluku history — from the age of spice empires, through VOC colonization, to independence and beyond. While spice trees were controlled and monopolized, the Kenari quietly sustained the people.

Ancient — Pre-Colonial

The Forest Provides

Indigenous Maluku communities have harvested Kenari from wild forests for generations. Integrated into daily food, medicine, and ceremony. The nut that fed the spice traders.

1600s — VOC Era

The Tree of Life Emerges

When the Dutch VOC enforced the Hongi voyages — destroying clove and nutmeg trees to control supply — Maluku families pivoted to Kenari. It became their economic lifeline: pohon kehidupan.

1945 — Independence

Cultural Identity Preserved

Kenari remains central to Maluku's culinary culture. Traditional preparations — Halua Kenari, Bagea, Air Guraka — are passed down through families, preserving flavor and knowledge together.

Today — NATSEPA

Premium. Global. Proud.

NATSEPA channels this deep heritage into modern premium products — processed at origin, certified Halal, BPOM registered — bringing Maluku's finest to the world market.

The Poga-Poga

The traditional harvesting tool of Maluku — a bamboo pole of 5 to 10 meters with a V-shaped fork at the tip, used to hook and shake Kenari fruits from trees that can tower 45 meters above the forest floor. No ladders. No machinery. Just skill.

THE PEOPLE

The Hands
Behind Every Nut.

NATSEPA is nothing without the people of Maluku. Over 200 farming families. Skilled craftspeople. And above all, the women who lead the selection and processing with unmatched precision.

Women-Led.
Community-Owned.

In Maluku's Kenari communities, women are the backbone of quality. They hand-select every kernel, identifying imperfections by touch and sight with a precision no machine has matched.

NATSEPA's commitment is to ensure that the value created from Maluku's forests stays in Maluku — in the hands of the people who tend, harvest, and craft with such care.

MALUKU CULTURE

The Kenari in
Maluku's Daily Life

For centuries, every part of the Kenari tree has been woven into the fabric of Maluku life — from food and medicine to ceremony and economy.

Kuliner
Halua Kenari

A beloved Maluku sweet — Kenari kernels glazed with palm sugar, still made by hand using ancestral recipes. The original NATSEPA inspiration.

Minuman
Air Guraka

Maluku's iconic ginger drink, topped with crushed Kenari. Served at every gathering — a symbol of hospitality and warmth in the Spice Islands.

Roti Tradisional
Bagea Kenari

Sago flour cookies enriched with Kenari — crisp, nutty, and deeply aromatic. The most famous Maluku oleh-oleh, inseparable from the Kenari's identity.

Hidangan Utama
Gohu Ikan

Ternate's raw tuna salad — a Maluku version of sashimi — served with crushed Kenari for richness and texture. Ancient, bold, unforgettable.

Obat Tradisional
Natural Medicine

Used in traditional medicine for rheumatism, inflammation, respiratory conditions, and as a natural energy booster. Science has since confirmed these benefits.

Ekosistem
Agroforestry Wisdom

Kenari trees were historically planted alongside nutmeg as natural windbreakers and shade providers — an ancient, sustainable agroforestry system still practiced today.
