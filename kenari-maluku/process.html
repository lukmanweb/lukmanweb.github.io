The Process

Five Steps.
One Year.
One Forest.

Every NATSEPA product is the result of a complete, unhurried journey. No shortcuts. No compromises. This is how it's done.

01 Step One

Wild
Harvested.

The Kenari tree (Canarium indicum) grows naturally in the primary rainforests of Maluku — on steep hillsides, deep valleys, and volcanic soil that no farm could replicate. These trees have never been planted by human hands. They are gifts of the forest.

Harvested once a year, at the exact moment of peak ripeness — determined by generations of local knowledge, not a calendar. Fruit that falls naturally to the forest floor is left to regenerate new trees, ensuring the cycle continues forever.

No planting. Kenari trees grow wild and have done so for hundreds of years. The oldest trees are estimated to be over a century old.
Traditional tool: Poga-Poga. A 5–10 meter bamboo pole with a V-shaped fork tip, used to hook and shake fruit from trees up to 45 meters tall.
Once per year. NATSEPA only harvests during the natural peak season — honoring the forest's rhythm rather than commercial demand.
02 Step Two

Careful
Selection.

After harvest, every batch of Kenari undergoes rigorous hand-sorting by local community members — primarily women — who have developed an extraordinary sensory precision through years of practice.

Kernels are evaluated by size, color, weight, and aroma. Only those meeting NATSEPA's premium standard proceed to the next stage. Rejected nuts are returned to the community for local consumption or composting — zero waste.

Visual inspection: Color consistency, shell integrity, and absence of mold or pest damage.
Touch assessment: Skilled hands detect hollow shells and inferior kernels by weight and texture — a skill no machine replicates.
Women-led quality. The selection process is primarily led by experienced women from local Maluku communities — the real guardians of Kenari quality.
03 Step Three

Hand
Cracked.

The Kenari shell is one of the hardest nut shells in nature — significantly harder than macadamia or walnut. It must be cracked open by hand using traditional wooden mallets and stone anvils in a precise technique developed over generations.

Industrial cracking machines destroy the delicate kernel inside and contaminate the natural oils. At NATSEPA, every single nut is opened by hand — slowly, carefully — to preserve the full, unbroken kernel and its precious fat content.

Traditional tools only. Wooden mallets and stone surfaces — the same tools Maluku families have used for centuries.
Kernel integrity matters. Whole, unbroken kernels retain more natural oils, better flavor, and superior nutritional value.
Slow, skilled work. An experienced cracker can open approximately 2kg of kernels per hour — each one requiring individual attention.
04 Step Four

Expertly
Crafted.

This is where the Kenari's character comes alive. Roasting at NATSEPA is never rushed — it is guided by smell, sound, and the color of the kernel. Each product variant receives its unique treatment:

Garlic Roast: Low-heat roasting with fresh Maluku garlic, added in layers to build depth without bitterness.
Cluster Binding: ALGARI kernels are carefully bound with a light natural starch coating, then slow-baked for maximum crunch.
Honey Glaze: Wild honey is heated and applied in careful stages, allowing each layer to caramelize before the next is added.
Arengga Glaze: Palm sugar is cooked to a precise temperature before coating — creating GLOWRY's signature toffee-caramel finish.
05 Step Five

Premium
Quality.

The final stage of every NATSEPA product is a multi-point quality check before packaging. Appearance, aroma, texture, and flavor are each assessed by our quality team against NATSEPA's exacting standards.

Packed in Maluku — at origin — ensuring maximum freshness and that every economic benefit remains in the hands of the community that created it. Certified, registered, and ready for the world.
